# trip.com promo code: Where working codes actually come from, and how to stack them with cashback on hotels, flights and car rentals

Most people searching for a Trip.com promo code get the same result: five sites claiming "80% OFF" with a copy box next to it. You paste the code at checkout, Trip.com says "invalid", and you book at full price anyway. The annoying part is that Trip.com genuinely does hand out big discounts — 6% to 20% off hotels and flights — but almost all of them come from banks, card networks and partners, not from coupon aggregators.

That distinction is the whole ballgame. Once you know which family a code belongs to, you can predict whether it will work before you waste a booking attempt on it.

## Why most Trip.com promo codes you find "don't work"

Trip.com promo codes aren't universal coupons. They're issued to a specific partner — a bank, a card network, a campaign page — and they're bound to that partner's terms. A code that gives an HSBC Hong Kong cardholder 8% off a hotel will do nothing for someone paying with an Indonesian debit card.

The recurring conditions, which show up across issuer terms from Visa, Mastercard, JCB and multiple Asian banks:

- **One promo code per order.** You cannot stack two codes, and you cannot combine a code with Trip Coins or other platform discounts. Pick whichever is bigger.
- **Partner codes often require a landing page.** Many are only valid if you arrived via the bank's designated campaign link, which is why a code that works for your friend fails for you.
- **Pay-at-hotel bookings are excluded.** Prepaid room types only.
- **China domestic flights are excluded** from most flight codes, and flights usually discount the fare before taxes, while hotels discount after taxes and fees.
- **Stock is capped.** Codes are first-come-first-served and campaigns close early when the budget runs out.
- **Currency matters.** A code valid for payment in HKD, MYR, IDR or THB generally won't apply in another currency.

If a coupon page doesn't tell you which card and currency a code needs, it's almost certainly recycled from somewhere else.

### 1. Trip.com's own campaign codes

Trip.com runs its own promo pages and coupon hubs, and these change constantly — seasonal campaigns, "Member Day", Monday-to-Thursday coupon drops, destination-specific codes such as Go Japan, Go Thailand or Go China. Codes from Trip.com's own Malaysian promo hub, for example, include **TRIPCAR8** for car rentals and **TRIPFLIGHT26SG** for flights, with redemptions capped and shown during checkout.

These are the easiest to use because there's no card condition attached, but the discounts are usually smaller than partner codes.

### 3. Aggregator codes — treat as a lottery ticket

Sites like Wethrift publish community-reported codes such as TK015, TT222, TRIPTRAIN2P or TT888, with notes like "last reported working 1 day ago". Some of these do work. They're also exactly the codes most likely to be expired, region-locked or recycled from a partner campaign. Nothing wrong with trying a couple at the payment page — it costs nothing — but don't plan your booking around them, and don't believe the "80% off" headline attached to a code that turns out to be 10% off select hotels.

## When the two routes conflict

There's one genuine catch, and it's worth knowing before you plan a booking around both.

Some bank and card codes are only valid if you arrived through that bank's designated Trip.com campaign page. ShopBack cashback is only valid if you arrived through ShopBack. You can't enter through two doors on the same order.

So on a large hotel booking, do the maths instead of guessing: a 20% Mastercard discount on a USD200 booking (capped at USD40) beats 16% cashback on the same booking. ## Questions that come up constantly

**Can I use a promo code and cashback on the same booking?**
Yes, in the sense that the code reduces what you pay and the cashback is calculated on the booking. What you can't do is arrive through two different partner links at once.

**Why did my code get rejected?**
The usual suspects: below minimum spend, ineligible product (China domestic flight, pay-at-hotel rate, some room types), expired or exhausted allocation, wrong payment currency, or a card-specific code paid with a different card.

**Do codes work on the app and the website?**
Generally both, and Trip.com sometimes runs app-only deals, so it's worth comparing the two before you commit.

**How long before cashback is actually payable?**
Pending within around 48 hours, Confirmed after the stay or flight completes. Withdrawal methods vary by market — PayPal, local bank transfer or FPS depending on where you are.

**Is a referral code the same as a promo code?**
No. A Trip.com promo code cuts your booking price. A cashback referral code is worth a signup bonus on the cashback side. They behave independently, which is why the stacking works.
